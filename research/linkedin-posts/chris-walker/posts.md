Google didn't fall in love with Reddit. It followed the users. People have been appending "reddit" to searches for years because they wanted answers that felt earned, not optimized.



After the May 2026 core update, Reddit's #1 rankings jumped 54%. That's the B2B signal.



Google is separating "needs expertise" from "needs experience." Your buyers need both.



Run this check: search your category, brand, and top competitors with "reddit" added. Look at pricing, onboarding, and switching stories. If Reddit says implementation is painful and your homepage says "launch in minutes," buyers are already noticing.



Stop treating Reddit as a side channel. It's buyer research, reputation monitoring, and content strategy in one place.

YouTube is the most underrated opportunity for B2B brands right now.



From a Foundation YouTube SERP analysis of 8k+ B2B SaaS keywords, YouTube shows up:

\- 80% of the time for demos

\- 22% of the time for "best \[category]"

\- 20% of the time for "\[brand] reviews"

\- 20% of the time for "\[brand] vs \[brand]"



Smart SaaS brands are doing two things:

1\. Optimizing old YouTube videos with relevant keywords, better thumbnails, and descriptions

2\. Creating new videos that rank for high priority keywords at scale



One Foundation client went from ranking for 150 keywords to over 15k just by embracing optimization. Don't sleep on YouTube.
