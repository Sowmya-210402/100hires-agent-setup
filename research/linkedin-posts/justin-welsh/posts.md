I can trace my success back to one specific kind of work:



Difficult work.



The stuff that was uncomfortable, scary, and hard enough that I was a different person by the time I finished it.



That doesn't mean grinding 24/7 or putting your family on hold.



It means that early in your career especially, hard work is the thing that actually moves you forward.



25 years into my career, I finally feel like I can ease up a little.



Not because the work got easier, but because I've systematized the lessons that took me thousands of hours to learn:



1\. How to experiment

2\. How to read signal

3\. Content-product alignment

4\. Time optimization

5\. Building compounding assets

6\. Knowing when to quit

7\. Identity clarity

I left a $550,000 job at 38 with no plan and very little runway.



By every measure that LinkedIn cares about, I was late.



Most people my age had a decade head start on their own business.



I had a huge burnout and a resignation letter.



The timeline you're panicking about is fake.



You can be lost at 22 and fine.

You can be broke at 28 and fine.

You can start over at 38 and be fine.



What mattered was getting a small number of things right and letting them compound.



Not faster. Just right.

Everyone says you can only have two of these.



Why?



If you do business right, you can:



1\. Have a lot of fun.

Making money isn't bad.
